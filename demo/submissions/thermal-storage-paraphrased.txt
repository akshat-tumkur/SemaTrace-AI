In a closed experimental enclosure, investigators warmed ten litres of stored water to sixty degrees Celsius before leaving it unheated for an hour and a half. Temperature readings were taken at five-minute intervals so that the rate of energy loss could be tracked.

To evaluate different insulating layers fairly, the group used matching tanks and an equal initial water temperature for every trial. Holding the experimental setup steady made the insulation a more plausible explanation for differences in how rapidly the water cooled.
